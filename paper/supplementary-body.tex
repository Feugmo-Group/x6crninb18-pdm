\section{Model derivation}
\label{sec:derivation}

This section gives the complete derivation of the model used in the main text.
The mechanistic framework is the Point Defect Model (PDM) formulation of
Li et al.\ (2020) for oxide growth in supercritical water, specialized here from
supercritical to subcritical high-temperature water and applied to the duplex
oxide on Nb-stabilized X6CrNiNb18-10 (AISI~347) under the exposure conditions of
Veile et al.\ (2024). Equation numbers of the form ``Eq.~($n$) of the
supercritical-water formulation'' refer to that source; boxed (displayed and
labeled) equations are the working forms used in this study.

\subsection{System, geometry, and species}
\label{sec:geometry}

The scale is duplex. Coordinates are taken with the origin at the
barrier-layer/outer-layer (bl/ol) interface:
\begin{equation}
\underbrace{\text{metal (m)}}_{x = L_{\mathrm{bl}}}
\;\Big|\;
\underbrace{\text{barrier layer (bl), } \mathrm{MO}_{\chi/2}}_{x = 0}
\;\Big|\;
\underbrace{\text{outer layer (ol), } \mathrm{MO}_{\delta/2}}_{x = -L_{\mathrm{ol}}}
\;\Big|\;
\text{high-temperature water (e)} .
\end{equation}
That is, the metal/barrier-layer (m/bl) interface sits at $x = L_{\mathrm{bl}}$,
the bl/ol interface at $x = 0$, and the ol/water interface at
$x = -L_{\mathrm{ol}}$.

The microstructural identification of the PDM objects for X6CrNiNb18-10 in
boiling-water-reactor hydrothermal water, following the phase analysis of
Veile et al.\ (2024), is given in Table~\ref{tab:layers}.

The Kr\"oger--Vink species in the barrier layer are: the cation vacancy
$V_M^{\chi\prime}$, the cation interstitial $M_i^{\chi+}$, the oxygen vacancy
$V_O^{\bullet\bullet}$, the oxide ion $O_O$, the lattice cation $M_M$, and the
electron $e'$. Constants: $\gamma = F/(RT)$; $\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}$,
$\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}$ are the molar volumes per cation of the barrier-layer
and outer-layer oxides; $\Omega_m$ is the molar volume of the metal; and
$\mathrm{PBR} = \Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}/\Omega_m$ is the Pilling--Bedworth ratio.

The environment corresponds to the conditions of Veile et al.\ (2024):
\begin{itemize}
\item $T = 513.15$~K (240$^\circ$C), $P = 7$~MPa, i.e.\ liquid water with density
$\rho \approx 0.8137$~kg/L (IAPWS-97).
\item Since $\rho \gg 0.1$~g/cm$^3$, the electrochemical-oxidation
(high-density) branch of the supercritical-water PDM formulation applies: this
is the classical condensed-water PDM limit of the model.
\item Dissolved oxygen $[\mathrm{O_2}] = 0.4$~ppm; ultrapure water with
conductivity 0.055~$\mu$S/cm.
\item Neutral pH at temperature: $\mathrm{p}K_w(240\,^\circ\mathrm{C}) \approx 11.2$,
hence $\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.6$.
\item Dimensionless relative volumetric concentrations
(Eqs.~(13) and (14) of the supercritical-water formulation), with reference
concentration $C^0 = 1$~mol/L:
\begin{align}
C_{\mathrm{H_2O}} &= \frac{1000\,\rho}{18.015\, C^0_{\mathrm{H_2O}}} \approx 45.2 , \\
C_{\mathrm{O}} &= \frac{[\mathrm{O_2}]_{\mathrm{ppm}}\,\rho \times 10^{-3}}{32.00\, C^0_{\mathrm{O}}}
\approx 1.02 \times 10^{-5} .
\end{align}
\item Potential $V$: open circuit (no polarization applied in the autoclave).
$V$ is set by the $\mathrm{O_2/H_2O}$ redox couple (the electrochemical
corrosion potential) and is constant over an exposure; it is therefore absorbed
into effective rate constants (Section~\ref{sec:reduction}). The open-circuit
closure $i_{\mathrm{pol}} = 0$ (Eq.~(46) of the supercritical-water
formulation) becomes relevant only if $V$ is made explicit (parametric studies
in the electrochemical corrosion potential).
\end{itemize}

Only Reaction~3 (growth) and Reactions~10/10$'$ (destruction) move the
barrier-layer interfaces; only Reaction~11 destroys the outer layer. These four
reactions define the thickness ordinary differential equations.

\subsection{Potential distribution}
\label{sec:potential}

The postulates of the supercritical-water formulation (its Section~2.1) are:
potential drops exist at the m/bl and bl/e interfaces; the field strength
$\varepsilon_f$ in the barrier layer is constant (buffered, independent of $V$
and $L_{\mathrm{bl}}$); and the bl/e drop is linear in $V$ and pH and
independent of $L_{\mathrm{bl}}$:
\begin{align}
\phi_{\mathrm{bl/e}} &= \alpha V + \beta\,\mathrm{pH} + \phi^0 ,
&&\text{(Eq.~(1) of the supercritical-water formulation)} \\
V &= \phi_{\mathrm{m/bl}} + \varepsilon_f L_{\mathrm{bl}} + \phi_{\mathrm{bl/e}} ,
&&\text{(Eq.~(2) of the supercritical-water formulation)} \\
\phi_{\mathrm{m/bl}} &= (1-\alpha) V - \varepsilon_f L_{\mathrm{bl}}
- \beta\,\mathrm{pH} - \phi^0 .
&&\text{(Eq.~(3) of the supercritical-water formulation)}
\end{align}
Here $\alpha$ is the polarizability of the bl/e interface
($\partial \phi_{\mathrm{bl/e}} / \partial V$);
$\beta = \partial \phi_{\mathrm{bl/e}} / \partial\,\mathrm{pH}$
($< 0$, typically $-0.005$ to $-0.05$~V); and $\phi^0$ is the drop at $V = 0$,
$\mathrm{pH} = 0$, $L_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0$. The porous outer layer carries no
potential drop (the cathodic reactions occur at the bl/ol interface via
$\mathrm{O_2}/\mathrm{H_2O}$ transport through pores; there is no remote
cathode current in an autoclave at open circuit).

\subsection{Rate constants (activated-complex theory with partial charges)}
\label{sec:rates}

Every interfacial rate constant takes the form
(Eq.~(8) of the supercritical-water formulation, with the derivation in
Appendix~A of Li et al.\ (2020)):
\begin{equation}
k_i = k_i^{0}\, \exp(a_i V)\, \exp(b_i L_{\mathrm{bl}})\, \exp(c_i\,\mathrm{pH}) ,
\label{eq:rateconstant}
\end{equation}
with the temperature dependence carried by the base standard rate constant
(Eq.~(9) of the supercritical-water formulation):
\begin{equation}
k_i^{00} = k_i^{00\prime}\,
\exp\!\left[ -\frac{\alpha_i\, \Delta G^0_{R_i}}{R}
\left( \frac{1}{T} - \frac{1}{T_0} \right) \right] .
\end{equation}
The coefficients (Table~1 of the supercritical-water formulation), with
$\alpha_i$ the transfer coefficient of Reaction~$i$, are given in
Table~\ref{tab:coeffs}.

The m/bl reactions inherit their $V$-, $L_{\mathrm{bl}}$-, and pH-dependence
from $\phi_{\mathrm{m/bl}}$; the bl/ol electrochemical reactions inherit theirs
from $\phi_{\mathrm{bl/e}}$, hence $b_i = 0$ there. The single most important
structural fact is
\begin{equation}
b_3 = -\alpha_3 \chi \gamma \varepsilon_f < 0 :
\end{equation}
the growth reaction decelerates exponentially with film thickness. This is
what generates direct-logarithmic/pseudo-parabolic kinetics and a finite
steady-state thickness.

The steady-state defect flux balances (rates equal at both interfaces for each
defect; Eqs.~(10)--(12) of the supercritical-water formulation) are
\begin{align}
k_1\, C_{V_M}^{L} &= k_4 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,n} + k_{4'} , \\
k_2 &= \left( k_5 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,n} + k_{5'} \right) C_{M_i}^{0} , \\
\frac{\chi}{2}\, k_3 &= \left( k_6 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,m}
+ k_7 C_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^{\,p} + k_8 C_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^{\,p} \right) C_{V_O}^{0} .
\end{align}
The kinetic orders are: $n$ (Reactions 4 and 5 in $C_{\mathrm{O}}$),
$m$ (Reaction 6 in $C_{\mathrm{O}}$), $p$ (Reactions 7--9 in
$C_{\mathrm{H_2O}}$), $q$ (Reaction 10 in $C_{\mathrm{O}}$),
$r$ (Reaction 11 in $C_{\mathrm{O}}$), and $s$ (Reaction $10'$ in the
$\mathrm{H^+}$ activity ratio). Li et al.\ (2020) assume $n = m = q = r = 1/2$.

\subsection{Thickness evolution equations (the governing ODEs)}
\label{sec:odes}

\subsubsection{Barrier layer}

Only the non-conservative reactions contribute
(Eq.~(16) of the supercritical-water formulation, with porosity correction
$\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}}/\rho_{\mathrm{bl}}$):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
= \Omega_{\mathrm{bl}} \left[ k_3 - k_{10} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,q}
- k_{10'} \left( C_{\mathrm{H^+}}/C^0_{\mathrm{H^+}} \right)^{s} \right]
\frac{\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{bl}}} .
\end{equation}
Substituting
$k_3 = k_3^{0} \exp(a_3 V) \exp(b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}}) \exp(c_3\,\mathrm{pH})$
at constant $V$ and pH (Eqs.~(21)--(23) of the supercritical-water
formulation) gives the working form
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
= A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, \exp( b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}} ) - C_{\mathrm{bl}}
\;}
\label{eq:blode}
\end{equation}
with
\begin{align}
A_{\mathrm{bl}} &= \Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}\, k_3^{0}\, \exp(a_3 V)\,
\exp(c_3\,\mathrm{pH})\, \frac{\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{bl}}} ,
&&\text{(Eq.~(22) of the supercritical-water formulation)}
\label{eq:Abl} \\
C_{\mathrm{bl}} &= \Omega_{\mathrm{bl}} \left[ k_{10} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,q}
+ k_{10'} \left( C_{\mathrm{H^+}}/C^0_{\mathrm{H^+}} \right)^{s} \right]
\frac{\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{bl}}} .
&&\text{(Eq.~(23) of the supercritical-water formulation)}
\label{eq:Cbl}
\end{align}
(The printed ``$k_9$'' in Eq.~(23) of the supercritical-water formulation is a
typographical error for $k_{10}$, as required by consistency with its
Eqs.~(16), (17), and (44); see Section~\ref{sec:errata}.)

The \textbf{closed-form solution} for constant $A_{\mathrm{bl}}$, $b_3$,
$C_{\mathrm{bl}}$ and initial condition $L_{\mathrm{bl}}(0) = L_0$
(Eqs.~(24) and (39) of the supercritical-water formulation) is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{bl}}(t) = L_0 - \frac{1}{b_3}
\ln\!\left[ 1 + \frac{A_{\mathrm{bl}}}{C_{\mathrm{bl}}}\, e^{b_3 L_0}
\left( e^{-b_3 C_{\mathrm{bl}} t} - 1 \right) \right]
- C_{\mathrm{bl}}\, t .
\label{eq:blclosed}
\end{equation}

The \textbf{steady state} ($\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}/\mathrm{d}t = 0$;
Eqs.~(17)--(20) of the supercritical-water formulation) is
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{bl,ss}} = \frac{1-\alpha}{\varepsilon_f}\, V
- \frac{\beta}{\varepsilon_f}\, \mathrm{pH}
- \frac{1}{\alpha_3 \chi \varepsilon_f \gamma}
\ln\!\left[ \frac{ k_{10} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,q}
+ k_{10'} \left( C_{\mathrm{H^+}}/C^0_{\mathrm{H^+}} \right)^{s} }{ k_3^{0} } \right] .
\end{equation}
$L_{\mathrm{bl,ss}}$ is linear in $V$ and pH, the PDM signature. In
ultrapure hydrothermal water (0.055~$\mu$S/cm, $\mathrm{pH} \approx 5.6$,
$C_{\mathrm{O}} \approx 10^{-5}$) the destruction terms are very small:
$C_{\mathrm{bl}} \to 0$, the steady state moves to very large $L$, and over a
finite window
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{bl}}(t) \approx L_0 - \frac{1}{b_3}
\ln\!\left( 1 - b_3 A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, e^{b_3 L_0}\, t \right)
\end{equation}
This is direct logarithmic growth, which over 72--480~h is numerically
indistinguishable from the parabolic $t^{0.5}$ law fitted by Veile et al.\
(their Cr layer: $n = 0.4964$, no saturation at 480~h). Quantifying this
equivalence is one of the results of the study.

The limiting-case map (what the data can select between) is:
\begin{itemize}
\item $C_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0$, $|b_3| L \ll 1$: linear growth,
$L \approx L_0 + A_{\mathrm{bl}} t$.
\item $C_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0$, $|b_3| L = O(1)$: direct-logarithmic
(pseudo-parabolic over a decade of $t$).
\item $C_{\mathrm{bl}} > 0$: logarithmic $\to$ saturation at
$L_{\mathrm{bl,ss}}$.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{Outer layer and the constant-volume constraint}

The outer layer grows from cations transmitted through the barrier layer
(fluxes $k_1 C_{V_M}^{L}$ via vacancies and $k_2$ via interstitials, deposited
by Reactions 4/4$'$/5/5$'$), plus bl$\to$ol conversion (Reactions 10/10$'$),
minus dissolution (Reaction 11)
(Eq.~(26) of the supercritical-water formulation):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
= \Omega_{\mathrm{ol}} \left[ k_1 C_{V_M}^{L} + k_2
+ k_{10} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,q}
+ k_{10'} \left( C_{\mathrm{H^+}}/C^0_{\mathrm{H^+}} \right)^{s}
- k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r} \right]
\frac{\rho^0_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{ol}}} .
\label{eq:olode-full}
\end{equation}

\textbf{Constant-volume growth} is imposed (marker experiments show that the
bl/ol interface stays at the original metal surface): the total metal
consumption rate equals the rate of barrier-layer advance into the metal
(Eqs.~(29)--(32) of the supercritical-water formulation):
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_m}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \Omega_m \left( k_1 C_{V_M}^{L} + k_2 + k_3 \right)
&&\text{(metal destruction, Reactions 1+2+3)} , \\
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}t} &= \Omega_{\mathrm{bl}} \left( k_3 - k_d \right) ,
&&k_d := k_{10} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,q}
+ k_{10'} \left( C_{\mathrm{H^+}}/C^0_{\mathrm{H^+}} \right)^{s} ,
\end{align}
where the metal, being dense, carries no porosity correction and the
barrier-layer rate is written with $\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}}/\rho_{\mathrm{bl}}
= 1$; setting $\mathrm{d}L_m/\mathrm{d}t = \mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}/\mathrm{d}t$
gives
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
k_1 C_{V_M}^{L} + k_2 + k_3 = \mathrm{PBR} \left( k_3 - k_d \right)
\;}
\qquad
\text{(Eq.~(32) of the supercritical-water formulation)} .
\label{eq:cv}
\end{equation}
For an n-type barrier layer (interstitials/oxygen vacancies dominate,
$k_1 C_{V_M}^{L} \ll k_2$) and $k_d \approx 0$
(Eq.~(33) of the supercritical-water formulation):
\begin{equation}
k_2 = (\mathrm{PBR} - 1)\, k_3 .
\end{equation}

Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:cv} into the outer-layer equation
\eqref{eq:olode-full}, the $k_d$ terms combine exactly,
$(\mathrm{PBR}-1) k_3 - \mathrm{PBR}\, k_d + k_d
= (\mathrm{PBR}-1)(k_3 - k_d)$, giving
(Eq.~(41) of the supercritical-water formulation)
\begin{equation}
\boxed{\;
\begin{aligned}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
&= \Omega_{\mathrm{ol}} \left[ (\mathrm{PBR}-1)(k_3 - k_d)
- k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r} \right] \\
&= (\mathrm{PBR}-1) \frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
- \Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}\, k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r}\,
\frac{\rho^0_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\rho_{\mathrm{ol}}}
\end{aligned}
\;}
\label{eq:olode}
\end{equation}
Retaining both porosity corrections instead would give the coefficient
$(\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}/\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}})
(\rho^0_{\mathrm{ol}}/\rho_{\mathrm{ol}})
(\mathrm{PBR} - \rho_{\mathrm{bl}}/\rho^0_{\mathrm{bl}})$, so the dense-oxide
convention, and not merely equal porosity between the layers, is what
reduces it to the form above. The convention affects the physical
quantities recovered from the fit, not the fitted parameters themselves.
With negligible barrier-layer destruction the thickness ratio is then fixed
by stoichiometry alone (Eqs.~(36) and (37) of the supercritical-water
formulation):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}
= (\mathrm{PBR}-1)\, \frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}}
=: \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}} .
\end{equation}

\textbf{Closed form.} Li et al.\ (2020) write it
(their Eqs.~(42)--(45)) with
$A_{\mathrm{ol}} = (\mathrm{PBR}-1)
(\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}/\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}) A_{\mathrm{bl}}$ and
$C_{\mathrm{ol}} = \Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}
[ (\mathrm{PBR}-1) k_d + k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r} ]$
(so that $\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}/\mathrm{d}t
= A_{\mathrm{ol}} \exp(b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}}) - C_{\mathrm{ol}}$):
\begin{align}
L_{\mathrm{ol}}(t) &= \frac{A_{\mathrm{ol}}}{A_{\mathrm{bl}} b_3}
\ln\!\left[ A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, e^{b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}}(t)} - C_{\mathrm{bl}} \right]
- C_{\mathrm{ol}}\, t + D_{\mathrm{ol}} ,
\label{eq:olclosed} \\
D_{\mathrm{ol}} &= -\frac{A_{\mathrm{ol}}}{A_{\mathrm{bl}} b_3}
\ln\!\left[ A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, e^{b_3 L_0} - C_{\mathrm{bl}} \right]
+ L_{\mathrm{ol}}(0) .
\end{align}
Because $\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}/\mathrm{d}t$ is an exact affine function of
$\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}/\mathrm{d}t$, the reduced system also integrates
trivially:
\begin{gather}
L_{\mathrm{ol}}(t) = \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}
\left( L_{\mathrm{bl}}(t) - L_0 \right) + C_x\, t + L_{\mathrm{ol}}(0) ,
\label{eq:olaffine} \\
\mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}} := (\mathrm{PBR}-1)
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}} ,
\qquad
C_x := -\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}\, k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r} \;(\le 0) \notag
\end{gather}
This is an independent verification target alongside Eq.~\eqref{eq:olclosed}.

The total scale thickness is
$L_{\mathrm{tot}} = L_{\mathrm{bl}} + L_{\mathrm{ol}}$. The weight gain (not
measurable for the cube specimens of Veile et al., kept for completeness;
Eq.~(28) of the supercritical-water formulation) is
$\mathrm{d}(\Delta w)/\mathrm{d}t
= r_{\mathrm{bl}} \rho_{\mathrm{bl}}\, \mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}/\mathrm{d}t
+ r_{\mathrm{ol}} \rho_{\mathrm{ol}}\, \mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}/\mathrm{d}t$,
with $r$ the oxygen mass fraction of each oxide; a closed seven-parameter form
is given in Eqs.~(48)--(56) of the supercritical-water formulation.

\subsubsection{Interfacial current (for extensions with explicit corrosion potential)}

The partial currents (Appendix~B of Li et al.\ (2020)) are
$i_1 = \chi F k_1 C_{V_M}^{L}$, $i_2 = \chi F k_2$, $i_3 = \chi F k_3$,
$i_4 = -\chi F k_4 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,n}$,
$i_5 = -\chi F k_5 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,n} C_{M_i}^{0}$,
$i_6 = -2 F k_6 C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,m} C_{V_O}^{0}$,
$i_7 = -2 F k_7 C_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^{\,p} C_{V_O}^{0}$,
$i_9 = -2 F k_9 C_{\mathrm{H_2O}}^{\,p}$,
$i_{4'} = (\delta-\chi) F k_{4'}$, etc.
At open circuit the sum of the partial currents vanishes
(Eq.~(46) of the supercritical-water formulation), which fixes
$V = V_{\mathrm{ocp}}$ given the $k_i^{0}$.

\subsection{Reduction to the identifiable apparent-parameter model}
\label{sec:reduction}

At open circuit, $V$ and pH are constant over an exposure, so
$\exp(a_3 V + c_3\,\mathrm{pH})$ is a constant multiplier: it cannot be
separated from $k_3^{0}$ by thickness-versus-time data alone. The observable
model is therefore the \textbf{apparent-parameter system} (identical in
structure to Table~5 of Li et al.\ (2020)):
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
&= A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, \exp(b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}}) - C_{\mathrm{bl}} ,
& L_{\mathrm{bl}}(0) &= L_0 ,
\label{eq:app-bl} \\
\frac{\mathrm{d}L_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\mathrm{d}t}
&= \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}
\left( A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, \exp(b_3 L_{\mathrm{bl}}) - C_{\mathrm{bl}} \right) + C_x ,
& L_{\mathrm{ol}}(0) &= L_{\mathrm{ol},0} ,
\label{eq:app-ol}
\end{align}
with $C_x := -\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}} k_{11} C_{\mathrm{O}}^{\,r} \le 0$
(outer-layer dissolution / further oxidation only).

The parameter vector is
\begin{multline}
\theta = \big\{
A_{\mathrm{bl}}~\mathrm{[nm/h]},\;
b_3~\mathrm{[1/nm]}\ (<0),\;
C_{\mathrm{bl}}~\mathrm{[nm/h]}\ (\ge 0),\\
\mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}~\mathrm{[-]}\ (>0),\;
C_x~\mathrm{[nm/h]}\ (\le 0),\\
L_0~\mathrm{[nm]}\ (>0),\;
L_{\mathrm{ol},0}~\mathrm{[nm]}\ (\ge 0)
\big\}
\end{multline}
That is 7 parameters and 2 ODEs against data at 3 times for 2 layers (plus a
Ni consistency check). The accepted model M4 fixes $C_{\mathrm{bl}} = C_x = 0$ and fits the
remaining five; the initial outer-layer thickness $L_{\mathrm{ol},0}$ is the
parameter whose addition resolves the degenerate ridge of M3 (main text,
Section~3.1).

Physical quantities are recovered after fitting via
\begin{align}
\varepsilon_f &= \frac{-b_3}{\alpha_3 \chi \gamma}
&&\text{[from } b_3 = -\alpha_3 \chi \gamma \varepsilon_f \text{]} , \\
k_3^{0} &= \frac{A_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}\, \exp(a_3 V + c_3\,\mathrm{pH})}
&&\text{[order of magnitude, given } \alpha_3, V \text{]} , \\
\mathrm{PBR} &= 1 + \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}\,
\frac{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}}} , \\
k_d &= \frac{C_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}} ,
\end{align}
with $\alpha_3 \in [0.1, 0.5]$ (Li et al.\ (2020) fit $\alpha_3 = 0.12$ for
HCM12A) and $\chi = 8/3$, $\gamma = F/RT = 22.6$~V$^{-1}$ at 513.15~K. Molar
volumes per cation:
$\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}} = 46.7/3 = 15.6$~cm$^3$/mol for $\mathrm{FeCr_2O_4}$
(3 cations);
$\Omega_{\mathrm{ol}} = 45.0/3 = 15.0$~cm$^3$/mol for $\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$;
$\Omega_m \approx 7.1$~cm$^3$/mol for the fcc stainless steel. These are
broadly consistent, as an independent cross-check rather than a
re-derivation, with the theoretical $\mathrm{PBR} = 2.05$
($\mathrm{FeCr_2O_4}$) and $2.10$ ($\mathrm{Fe_3O_4}$) reported in
Table~3 of Li et al.\ (2020) for the same phases against their own
representative steel molar volume; the fit itself recovers
$\mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}} = 1.096$, the apparent
outer-to-barrier growth-rate ratio identified from the thickness data
(not $\Omega_{\mathrm{bl}}/\Omega_m$ directly), related to the physical
PBR through the equation above.

Priors/constraints (3 time points cannot determine 7 parameters freely).
The only prior applied in every fit is the Gaussian log-space prior of
Eq.~(16) of the main text: $\ln L_0 \sim
\mathcal{N}(\ln 2~\mathrm{nm},\, 0.60^2)$, whose $\pm2\sigma$ range is
0.6--6.6~nm, matching the air-formed-film range below.

Earlier versions of this analysis also applied
$\ln \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}} \sim \mathcal{N}(\ln 1.05,\, 0.20^2)$,
on the grounds that 1.05 was the stoichiometric value. It is not: the
constant-volume closure giving 1.05 requires the barrier layer to draw
$\approx 30$~at\% Cr from an 18.8~at\% alloy, and a chromium mass balance
with an $\mathrm{FeCr_2O_4}$ barrier gives 2.05 when all non-Ni iron
reports to the outer layer. That prior has been removed;
$\mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}$ is now determined by the likelihood alone
(1.096 for M4). The apparent gap between 1.096 and 2.05 is examined in
the main text and does not survive: the mass-balance figure is
phase-dependent and spans $[0.52, 3.79]$ over the candidate barrier
spinels, and once an outer-layer loss term is admitted the FeCr$_2$O$_4$
value is reached at no cost in $\chi^2$. The physical ranges below
motivate the remaining prior:
\begin{itemize}
\item $L_0$: air-formed film on high-Cr austenitic stainless steel, 1--5~nm
(Li et al.\ (2020): 1.01~nm for 316L).
\item $\mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}$: no prior; fitted freely. The
chromium mass balance admits $[0.52, 3.79]$ depending on the barrier
phase assignment; the likelihood returns 1.096, inside that range.
\item $C_{\mathrm{bl}}$: $\ge 0$, expected $\approx 0$ (ultrapure water); both
$C_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0$ (five-parameter model) and free $C_{\mathrm{bl}}$ are
tested.
\item $b_3$: strictly $< 0$. $|b_3| \sim \alpha_3 \chi \gamma \varepsilon_f$;
with $\varepsilon_f \in [10^2, 10^6]$~V/cm this spans
$|b_3| \in [\sim 7 \times 10^{-5}, \sim 0.7]$~nm$^{-1}$: the data must localize it.
\item $C_x$: $\le 0$ by construction, and small.
\end{itemize}

The units convention for the implementation is thickness in nm and time in h.
Conversions from the cgs units (cm, s) of Li et al.\ (2020):
1~cm/s $= 3.6 \times 10^{10}$~nm/h; 1~cm$^{-1}$ $= 10^{-7}$~nm$^{-1}$.
Sign conventions respected throughout: $b_3 < 0$, $C_{\mathrm{bl}} \ge 0$,
$\beta < 0$, $D_{\mathrm{ol}} < 0$.

\subsection{Numerically stable evaluation of the closed forms}
\label{sec:logspace}

Equation~\eqref{eq:blclosed} is not evaluated as written. For
$C_{\mathrm{bl}} \to 0$ the ratio $A_{\mathrm{bl}}/C_{\mathrm{bl}}$ diverges
while the bracket it multiplies vanishes, and in the fitted regime
($C_{\mathrm{bl}}$ driven to zero by the data) a direct evaluation loses all
significance. The implementation therefore takes the $C_{\mathrm{bl}} = 0$
branch analytically,
\begin{equation}
L_{\mathrm{bl}}(t) = L_0 - \frac{1}{b_3}
\operatorname{log1p}\!\left(-b_3 A_{\mathrm{bl}} e^{\,b_3 L_0}\, t\right) ,
\end{equation}
and for $C_{\mathrm{bl}} > 0$ forms the logarithm of the bracket in log space.
Writing $z = -b_3 C_{\mathrm{bl}} t \ge 0$ and $u = 1 + (A_{\mathrm{bl}} /
C_{\mathrm{bl}})e^{\,b_3 L_0}(e^{z}-1)$, the identity
$e^{z} - 1 = e^{z}(1 - e^{-z})$ gives
\begin{equation}
\ln(u-1) = \ln A_{\mathrm{bl}} - \ln C_{\mathrm{bl}} + b_3 L_0 + z
+ \operatorname{log1p}\!\left(-e^{-z}\right) ,
\qquad
\ln u = \operatorname{logaddexp}(0, \ln(u-1)) ,
\end{equation}
in which every term is evaluated by a library routine that is exact in its own
small-argument limit. The result is accurate in both the $|b_3| L \ll 1$ and
the $|b_3| L = O(1)$ regimes, which is what the parity check of
Section~\ref{sec:verification} confirms and what makes a 1000-member bootstrap
over the closed form affordable.

\subsection{Nondimensionalization for the neural spectral-element solver}
\label{sec:nondim}

The characteristic scales are $t_c = 480$~h (the exposure window) and
$L_c = 100$~nm (the observed Cr-layer scale). With $\tau = t/t_c$ and
$\lambda = L/L_c$:
\begin{align}
\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\mathrm{bl}}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}
&= \hat{A}\, \exp( \hat{b}\, \lambda_{\mathrm{bl}} ) - \hat{C} ,
& \lambda_{\mathrm{bl}}(0) &= \lambda_0 , \\
\frac{\mathrm{d}\lambda_{\mathrm{ol}}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}
&= \mathrm{PBR}_{\mathrm{eff}}
\left( \hat{A}\, \exp( \hat{b}\, \lambda_{\mathrm{bl}} ) - \hat{C} \right)
+ \hat{C}_x ,
\end{align}
with
\begin{equation}
\hat{A} = \frac{A_{\mathrm{bl}}\, t_c}{L_c} , \qquad
\hat{b} = b_3 L_c , \qquad
\hat{C} = \frac{C_{\mathrm{bl}}\, t_c}{L_c} , \qquad
\hat{C}_x = \frac{C_x\, t_c}{L_c} , \qquad
\lambda_0 = \frac{L_0}{L_c} .
\end{equation}
For the expected regime ($L_{\mathrm{bl}}$: 37 $\to$ 134~nm over 72 $\to$
480~h with near-parabolic shape) these groups are $O(0.1$--$10)$: e.g.\ a
pure-logarithmic fit gives $\hat{b} \approx -2 \ldots -4$,
$\hat{A} \approx O(10)$, $\hat{C} \approx 0$. All learnable parameters are $O(1)$; a badly scaled parameter is an unlearnable parameter, so this scaling is applied from the start.

The residuals are enforced in Crank--Nicolson integral form on the
Gauss--Lobatto--Legendre time grid (otherwise the conditioning of the spectral
first-derivative matrix is the dominant error floor):
\begin{equation}
R_j = \lambda_j - \lambda_{j-1}
- \frac{\Delta t_j}{2} \left( f(\lambda_{j-1}) + f(\lambda_j) \right) ,
\qquad j = 1, \ldots, N_t - 1 ,
\end{equation}
plus the initial-condition residuals $\lambda_{\mathrm{bl}}(0) - \lambda_0$
and $\lambda_{\mathrm{ol}}(0)$.

\section{Spatial defect-transport model}
\label{sec:spatial-derivation}

This section gives the complete governing equations, boundary conditions,
and initial condition for the spatial extension of Section~3.4 of the main
text, which resolves oxygen- and cation-vacancy transport across the
barrier layer under the field strength identified in
Section~\ref{sec:derivation}. Nothing here is fit to the spatial data;
every kinetic quantity entering this section (the growth prefactor, field
exponent, and barrier-layer thickness $L(t)$) is frozen at its
zero-dimensional value.

\subsection{Species, flux, and nondimensionalization}

Two defect species are resolved on the barrier layer, $x \in [0, L]$ with
the origin at the metal/barrier-layer interface: oxygen vacancies
$c_\text{OV}(x,t)$ (charge $z = +2$, generated at the metal/barrier-layer
interface by the growth reaction and annihilated at the
barrier/outer-layer interface, migrating outward) and cation vacancies
$c_\text{CV}(x,t)$ (charge $z = -\chi$ with $\chi = 8/3$ the
spinel-averaged cation valence, generated at the barrier/outer-layer
interface and annihilated at the metal/barrier-layer interface, migrating
inward). Each species obeys a Nernst--Planck flux under the constant
field $\varepsilon_f$ identified in the zero-dimensional fit, with
Einstein mobility $u = D\gamma$, $\gamma = F/(RT)$:
\begin{equation}
J = -D\,\frac{\partial c}{\partial x} + z\gamma\varepsilon_f D\,c.
\label{eq:nernstplanck}
\end{equation}
Nondimensionalizing depth as $\xi = x/L$, concentration as
$\hat{c} = c/c_0$ with the migration scale
$c_0 = |J|/|v|$, $v = z\gamma\varepsilon_f D$ (the migration velocity),
and flux as $\hat{J} = J/(|v|c_0) = \operatorname{sign}(v)$, the steady
equation becomes
\begin{equation}
\frac{d}{d\xi}\!\left[-\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}}\frac{d\hat{c}}{d\xi} + s\hat{c}\right] = 0,
\qquad
\mathrm{Pe} = |z|\gamma\varepsilon_f L, \qquad s = \operatorname{sign}(z).
\label{eq:steadypde}
\end{equation}
As discussed in the main text, the diffusivity $D$ cancels from the
P\'eclet number $\mathrm{Pe}$: the nondimensional steady profile shape
depends only on quantities the zero-dimensional fit already fixes, and
$D$ enters only the dimensional concentration scale $c_0$ (invisible to
a composition measurement such as EDX).

\subsection{Boundary conditions}

Both species obey a Robin (interfacial-kinetics) boundary condition at
their annihilation interface, with $\kappa$ the ratio of the interfacial
rate constant to the migration velocity:
\begin{align}
\text{OV (annihilation at } \xi=1\text{):}\quad & J = k_a\,c(L) \;\Longrightarrow\; \hat{c}(1) = 1/\kappa_a,
\label{eq:bc-ov}\\
\text{CV (annihilation at } \xi=0\text{):}\quad & |J| = k_1\,c(0) \;\Longrightarrow\; \hat{c}(0) = 1/\kappa_1.
\label{eq:bc-cv}
\end{align}
The generation flux at the opposite interface is anchored to the
zero-dimensional growth kinetics rather than independently fit: the
oxygen-vacancy generation flux is $J_\text{OV} = (\chi/2)\,k_3(L)$ with
$k_3(L)$ the barrier-layer growth rate constant at thickness $L$
(mol\,cm$^{-2}$\,s$^{-1}$, converted from the fitted $dL_\mathrm{bl}/dt$),
and the cation-vacancy generation flux is anchored to it by an assumed
ratio, $|J_\text{CV}| = r_\text{CV} J_\text{OV}$.

The parameters $\kappa_a$, $\kappa_1$, $r_\text{CV}$, and the two
diffusivities $D_\text{OV}$, $D_\text{CV}$ are \emph{assumed}, not
identified: per the synthetic identifiability gate of Section~3.4 of the main text, three exposure times cannot constrain interfacial transport
rate constants, so these are treated as forward-only inputs, documented
here for reproducibility rather than reported as findings. The values
used throughout are $\varepsilon_f = 1.73\times10^4$~V/cm (the
zero-dimensional fit), $\Omega_\mathrm{bl} = 15.6$~cm$^3$/mol,
$D_\text{OV} = 10^{-15}$~cm$^2$/s, $D_\text{CV} = 10^{-16}$~cm$^2$/s,
$\kappa_a = \kappa_1 = 1.25$, and $r_\text{CV} = 0.5$.

\subsection{Analytic steady solution}

Because the nondimensional flux is constant in steady state
($\hat{J} = s$ everywhere), Eq.~\eqref{eq:steadypde} admits an exact
closed-form solution: a uniform plateau at $\hat{c} = 1$ (the particular
solution) plus a homogeneous exponential mode that satisfies the Robin
condition at the annihilation boundary $\xi_\text{bc} \in \{0, 1\}$:
\begin{equation}
\hat{c}(\xi) = 1 + \left(\hat{c}_\text{bc} - 1\right)
\exp\!\big[s\,\mathrm{Pe}\,(\xi - \xi_\text{bc})\big].
\label{eq:steadyanalytic}
\end{equation}
This is the reference solution the Newton and NSEM steady solves are
verified against (Section~\ref{sec:baseline}).

\subsection{Transient (moving-boundary) problem}

The transient problem is solved on a Landau-transformed fixed domain
$\xi \in [0,1]$ that tracks the growing barrier-layer thickness $L(t)$,
with $\tau = t/t_c$ the nondimensional time ($t_c = 480$~h, the exposure
window). Writing $\mathrm{Pe}(\tau) = |z|\gamma\varepsilon_f L(\tau)$,
$\hat{\jmath}(\tau) = k_3(L(\tau))/k_3(L_\text{ref})$ the growth-flux
ratio to a reference thickness $L_\text{ref} = L(\tau{=}1)$,
$\mathrm{adv}(\tau) = d\ln L/d\tau$ the Landau advection strength, and
$\mathrm{mig}(\tau) = t_c/\tau_\text{mig}(L(\tau))$ the flux-divergence
prefactor ($\tau_\text{mig} = L/|v|$ the migration transit time), the
governing equation is
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial \hat{c}}{\partial \tau}
= \xi\,\mathrm{adv}(\tau)\,\frac{\partial \hat{c}}{\partial \xi}
- \mathrm{mig}(\tau)\,\frac{\partial \hat{J}}{\partial \xi},
\qquad
\hat{J} = -\frac{1}{\mathrm{Pe}(\tau)}\frac{\partial \hat{c}}{\partial \xi} + s\hat{c}.
\label{eq:transientpde}
\end{equation}
\textbf{Boundary conditions} (per time node): at the generation
boundary, $\hat{J} = s\,\hat{\jmath}(\tau)$ (the zero-dimensional
growth-flux anchor); at the annihilation boundary,
$\hat{J} = s\,\kappa\,\hat{c}$ (the same Robin closure as the steady
problem). \textbf{Initial condition}: the instantaneous quasi-steady
profile at $\tau_0$, $\hat{c}(\xi, \tau_0) = \hat{\jmath}(\tau_0)\,\hat{c}_\text{ss}(\xi;\mathrm{Pe}(\tau_0))$,
with $\hat{c}_\text{ss}$ the analytic steady shape of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:steadyanalytic}. Quasi-steadiness is quantified in
Section~\ref{sec:qs} by comparing the transient solution at $\tau=1$
against the instantaneous quasi-steady profile at that same moment.

\subsection{Synthetic-EDX instrument and heterogeneity model for the
identifiability gate}
\label{sec:instrument}

The synthetic identifiability gate of the main text (20 replicate
experiments per candidate parameter set) generates each replicate with
the following instrument and heterogeneity model, anchored to the
scan-to-scan scatter of Veile et al.'s replicate line scans:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Spatial sampling:} composition profiles sampled every
2~nm over $x \in [-120, 220]$~nm (170 points per scan), four element
traces (O, Cr, Fe, Ni) per scan, three scans per replicate experiment.
\item \textbf{EDX spatial resolution:} Gaussian smoothing with
$\sigma = 2/2.355 \approx 0.85$~nm (2~nm full width at half maximum).
\item \textbf{Per-scan position jitter:} each scan is rigidly shifted
by a Gaussian offset with $\sigma = 0.945$~nm.
\item \textbf{Compositional noise:} independent Gaussian noise per
point, with standard deviation $\max(0.02\,|v|,\ 0.2)$ weight
percent, that is 2\% relative, floored at 0.2 absolute weight
percent.
\item \textbf{Scan-to-scan physical heterogeneity:} each scan draws its
own realization of the shape parameters from log-normal distributions
with per-parameter coefficients of variation $L_\mathrm{bl}$: 0.04,
$w_\text{int}$: 0.30, $w_\text{out}$: 0.30, $A_\text{Ni}$: 0.15,
$w_\text{Ni}$: 0.14, $f_\text{Cr,bl}$: 0.05, $g_\mathrm{bl}$: 0.30,
calibrated to the replicate-scan scatter of Veile et al.'s Fig.~9.
Omitting this heterogeneity term (treating instrument noise as the only
uncertainty source) produces a spuriously optimistic identifiability
verdict for every parameter.
\item \textbf{Verdict thresholds:} a parameter is classed identifiable
if the relative standard deviation of its 20 replicate recoveries is
below 0.25, weakly identifiable below 0.50, and unidentifiable
otherwise. The systematic (accuracy, as opposed to precision) error of
EDX quantification, of order $\pm10\%$ relative, is not simulated; it
floors the absolute-composition parameters $f_\text{Cr,bl}$ and
$A_\text{Ni}$ independently of the statistical verdict.
\end{itemize}

\section{Nickel-exclusion moving-frame model}
\label{sec:ni-model}

The nickel enrichment zone at the receding metal/barrier-layer interface
(Section~3.5 of the main text) is modeled as Wagner-type solute
enrichment ahead of a moving boundary, in the interface-attached frame
$\xi$ (depth into the metal ahead of the interface, $\xi \ge 0$):
\begin{equation}
\frac{\partial c}{\partial t} = D_\text{Ni,eff}\,\frac{\partial^2 c}{\partial \xi^2}
+ v_m(t)\,\frac{\partial c}{\partial \xi},
\label{eq:wagner}
\end{equation}
where the metal streams toward $\xi=0$ at the combined interface
recession speed
\begin{equation}
v_m(t) = \frac{1}{\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{bl}}\frac{dL_\mathrm{bl}}{dt}
+ \phi_\text{ol,supply}\,\frac{1}{\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{ol}}\frac{dL_\mathrm{ol}}{dt},
\label{eq:vm}
\end{equation}
where $\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{bl} := \Omega_\mathrm{bl}/\Omega_m$ and
$\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{ol} := \Omega_\mathrm{ol}/\Omega_m$ are the
phase-specific Pilling--Bedworth ratios of the barrier and outer
layers against the metal (distinct from the single unsubscripted
$\mathrm{PBR}$ of Section~\ref{sec:derivation}, which refers to the
barrier layer only). Numerically $\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{bl} = 2.05$ and
$\mathrm{PBR}_\mathrm{ol} = 2.10$ (FeCr$_2$O$_4$ and Fe$_3$O$_4$
molar-volume ratios, Li et al.\ (2020), Table~3) and both layer growth
rates are taken from the frozen zero-dimensional kinetics. The free parameter $\phi_\text{ol,supply}$ is
the fraction of the outer layer's cation supply drawn from the matrix
through the barrier layer (as opposed to re-precipitation of
already-dissolved species); $r_\text{Ni}$, the nickel incorporation
ratio, enters through the boundary condition below.

\textbf{Boundary condition} (rejection flux at the moving interface,
$\xi=0$):
\begin{equation}
D_\text{Ni,eff}\,\frac{\partial c}{\partial \xi}\bigg|_{\xi=0}
= -v_m(t)\,(1 - r_\text{Ni})\,c(0,t),
\label{eq:ni-bc}
\end{equation}
which in the steady limit reduces to the classical rejection result
$c(0) = c_m/r_\text{Ni}$. \textbf{Far-field condition}:
$c(\xi \to \infty, t) = c_m$, the bulk matrix nickel content
($c_m = 10$~wt\%). \textbf{Initial condition}: $c(\xi, 0) = c_m$
everywhere (no pre-existing enrichment).

The model is solved by method-of-lines finite differencing on
$\xi \in [0, 300]$~nm with 150 nodes and Radau time integration
(relative tolerance $10^{-8}$, absolute tolerance $10^{-10}$), not by
the NSEM machinery, since the three free parameters
($D_\text{Ni,eff}$, $r_\text{Ni}$, $\phi_\text{ol,supply}$) are fit
directly to four observables (zone widths at 72/168/480~h and the 72~h
peak amplitude) by nonlinear least squares. Observed features are
extracted from the solved profile using Veile et al.'s own convention:
zone width $W(t)$ is the extent over which $c > c_m + 2$~wt\%, and
amplitude $A(t) = c(0,t)$.

\subsection{Fitted values under the rejected constant-mobility model}
\label{sec:ni-fit}

The constant-mobility model above is rejected by the data:
$\chi^2 = 25.71$ on one degree of freedom, with the discrepancy in the
\emph{shape} of $W(t)$ (predicted monotonically growing, measured
approximately flat). Because parameter estimates and nested-model
$\Delta\chi^2$ comparisons obtained under a structurally rejected model
are not interpretable as estimates or as evidence, the fitted values are
recorded here for completeness and reproducibility only, and are not used
to support any claim in the main text. Table~\ref{tab:ni-fit} gives them.

\section{NSEM implementation and training details}
\label{sec:nsem-details}

This section documents every equation, loss term, boundary/initial
condition, and hyperparameter of the four NSEM training configurations
used in this paper, at the level of detail needed to reproduce them
independently of the released code.

\subsection{Shared representation}

Every configuration represents its field(s) on a discrete-variational-%
representation (DVR) collocation node set (Gauss--Lobatto--Legendre
nodes, optionally log-clustered toward one end via a compression
parameter $\alpha$) using an element network with hidden width 32 and 3
layers, either a standard multilayer perceptron or a Kolmogorov--Arnold
network backbone (polynomial degree 4). Thickness fields are passed
through a softplus nonlinearity plus a floor of $10^{-6}$ to guarantee
strict positivity. All training uses double-precision (float64)
arithmetic.

\subsection{Configuration 1: zero-dimensional forward solve}
\label{sec:config-forward}

Two time-only element networks, $\lambda_\mathrm{bl}(\tau)$ and
$\lambda_\mathrm{ol}(\tau)$ (nondimensional barrier- and outer-layer
thickness), on $N_t = 24$ DVR nodes over $\tau \in [0, 1]$
(log-clustered, compression $\alpha_t = 1.5$, mapping toward $\tau=0$).
Training proceeds in two stages: (1) an initial-condition pretrain
(800 Adam steps at learning rate $10^{-2}$ plus an L-BFGS polish) that
drives both networks to their constant initial-condition values,
$\lambda_\mathrm{bl} = \lambda_0$ and $\lambda_\mathrm{ol} = \lambda_{\mathrm{ol},0}$
everywhere; (2) physics training against the Crank--Nicolson residuals
of the nondimensional ODE system of Section~\ref{sec:derivation}, with
three loss terms (\texttt{ode\_bl}, \texttt{ode\_ol}, \texttt{ic})
combined by the BRDR aggregator (equal initial weights, adaptively
rebalanced during training) and optimized for 2000 Adam steps
(learning rate $10^{-3}$) followed by 3 L-BFGS phases (max.\ 400
iterations each, strong-Wolfe line search, gradient/step tolerances
$10^{-13}$/$10^{-15}$). The initial-condition loss carries a fixed
multiplier $\lambda_\text{ic} = 10$. Verification: relative
$L^\infty$ error against the closed-form solution, acceptance
$\le 0.5\%$.

\subsection{Configuration 2: zero-dimensional inverse, hard mode}
\label{sec:config-hard}

The kinetic parameters
$\{A_\mathrm{bl}, b_3, \mathrm{PBR}_\text{eff}, L_0, L_{\mathrm{ol},0}\}$
(optionally $C_\mathrm{bl}$) are the only learnable quantities,
parameterized in log space (negative-log for $b_3$) to guarantee sign
and positivity by construction. The thickness trajectories are
generated by differentiable fourth-order Runge--Kutta integration of
the nondimensional ODE (8 sub-steps per grid interval) through these
parameters, then the affine outer-layer relation, so the governing
equations are satisfied exactly and only a data-plus-prior loss is
minimized:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{hard} =
\ell_\text{data}(\lambda_\mathrm{bl}; t_\text{Cr}, \bar{L}_\text{Cr}, \sigma_\text{Cr})\, n_\text{Cr}
+ \ell_\text{data}(\lambda_\mathrm{ol}; t_\text{Fe}, \bar{L}_\text{Fe}, \sigma_\text{Fe})\, n_\text{Fe}
+ \lambda_\text{prior}\Big(\tfrac{\ln L_0-\ln(2/L_c)}{0.60}\Big)^{\!2},
\label{eq:hardloss}
\end{equation}
where $\ell_\text{data}$ is the mean squared, error-normalized
misfit between the network trajectory (interpolated to the data times)
and the observations, $n_\text{Cr}=n_\text{Fe}=3$ are the exposure-time
counts (matching the main-text $\chi^2$ normalization), and
$\lambda_\text{prior}=1$. Optimized with 4000 Adam steps
(learning rate $10^{-2}$) followed by one L-BFGS phase (max.\ 400
iterations). This is the reference inverse mode for the
zero-dimensional model; the real-data run initializes at $2\times$ the
reference parameter values (\texttt{init\_scale}=2.0) to verify
convergence is not an artifact of a favorable initialization.

\subsection{Configuration 3: zero-dimensional inverse, soft mode}
\label{sec:config-soft}

Identical field representation and pretraining to
Section~\ref{sec:config-forward} (kinetics frozen during pretrain;
pretrain targets are \emph{linear ramps} from the initial condition to
the last data point, not constants, so the kinetic parameters receive a
nonzero time-variation gradient once joint training begins), then joint
training of the field networks and the kinetic parameters against six
loss terms combined by BRDR:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{L}_\text{soft} =
\lambda_\text{phys}\big(\ell_\text{ode,bl} + \ell_\text{ode,ol}\big)
+ \lambda_\text{ic}\,\ell_\text{ic}
+ \lambda_\text{data}\big(\ell_\text{data,bl} + \ell_\text{data,ol}\big)
+ \lambda_\text{prior}\,\ell_\text{prior},
\label{eq:softloss}
\end{equation}
with $\lambda_\text{phys} = 1000$ (fixed multiplier on the ODE
residual terms; without it the joint optimum leaves residual slack
that is absorbed into the trajectory instead of the kinetics, the origin of failure mode F-1), $\lambda_\text{ic} = 10$,
$\lambda_\text{data} = 100$, and $\lambda_\text{prior} = 1$. The two
exhibits reported in the main text (Table~6) use
$\lambda_\text{data}=10$ as an independent reweighting check. Optimized
with 4000 Adam steps (learning rate $10^{-3}$) and 3 L-BFGS phases
(max.\ 400 iterations each). The self-consistency check re-solves the
recovered kinetic parameters through the exact closed-form/RK4 forward
solve and compares the resulting $\chi^2$ to the trajectory $\chi^2$;
consistency is declared satisfied if
$\chi^2_\text{exact} < \max(2\chi^2_\text{traj},\, \chi^2_\text{traj}+2)$.
The constants in this acceptance rule are a working heuristic (tolerate
up to a doubling, or an absolute slack of two $\chi^2$ units for
near-zero trajectory misfits), not a derived statistical threshold; the
failure-mode exhibits in the main text exceed it by more than an order
of magnitude, so the qualitative verdict does not depend on the
specific constants. The specific $\chi^2$ values of a soft-mode run
vary from training run to training run (an earlier run of the
$\lambda_\text{data}=10$ configuration landed at trajectory
$\chi^2 = 0.004$ against closed-form $\chi^2 = 47.3$), but the
orders-of-magnitude inconsistency signature is stable across runs; the
main text quotes single-seed (seed~0) values.

\subsection{Configuration 4: spatial extension (steady and transient)}
\label{sec:config-spatial}

\textbf{Steady} (Section~\ref{sec:spatial-derivation}): a single field network
on $N_x = 24$ DVR nodes, uniformly spaced on $\xi \in [0,1]$ ($\alpha_x = 0$;
the P\'eclet numbers in this problem, 10--14, produce only mild boundary layers,
so uniform spacing suffices). The barrier-layer thickness is fixed at its 480-h
value from the zero-dimensional fit. The loss adds the first-integral form of
the steady equation, $(\hat{J}-s)^2$ averaged over all nodes, which penalizes
flux non-conservation directly rather than differentiating a second-derivative
residual, to the squared Robin-boundary violation at the annihilation node,
trained for 2000 Adam steps (learning rate $10^{-3}$) after a 500-step pretrain
to the boundary value, then L-BFGS polish. The independent Newton reference
solves the same first-integral system on the identical node set by Newton
iteration (converges in one step, since the discrete system is linear; verified
to residual $<10^{-13}$).

\textbf{Transient}: field network on the tensor-product grid of the
same $N_x=24$ spatial DVR nodes and $N_t=16$ temporal DVR nodes
($\alpha_t=1.5$, log-clustered, $\tau \in [\tau_0, 1]$ with
$\tau_0=0.05$). Crank--Nicolson residuals of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:transientpde} are combined with the two per-time-node
boundary residuals and the initial-condition residual (four loss terms:
\texttt{pde}, \texttt{bc\_gen}, \texttt{bc\_ann}, \texttt{ic}), BRDR-%
aggregated and optimized identically to the steady case.

\section{Quasi-steady approximation error}
\label{sec:qs}

The interface-reaction-controlled model of Section~\ref{sec:derivation} treats
the defect concentration profiles inside the barrier layer as quasi-steady.
Figure~\ref{fig:transient} quantifies the error incurred by this assumption
using a full moving-boundary transport solve. At the end of the 480~h exposure
window, the oxygen-vacancy species (whose migration transit time across the film
is approximately 5~h) lags its quasi-steady profile shape by only 0.69\%, while
the slower cation-vacancy species (transit time approximately 36~h) lags by
5.79\%. The quasi-steady closure is therefore an excellent approximation for the
species that controls film growth, and an acceptable one for the slowest defect
over the experimental window.

\section{Neural-backbone parity}
\label{sec:backbone}

The inverse-identification results in the main text are independent of the
choice of neural backbone. Trained on the forward problem and compared against
the closed-form solutions of Section~\ref{sec:derivation}, the multilayer
perceptron (MLP) backbone achieves relative $L^\infty$ errors of 0.14\% for
the barrier layer and 0.12\% for the outer layer, while the
Kolmogorov--Arnold backbone achieves 0.11\% and 0.04\%, respectively. Both
backbones are well inside the 0.5\% acceptance bar, so the parameter estimates
and uncertainty statements reported in the main text do not depend on the
backbone architecture.
